Verizon

Home Internet · 2024 to 2026

Simplifying plan selection

Role
Principal Designer
Timeline
Q1 2026
Team
Product, Engineering, Design System

Verizon Home Internet has about 17 million customers and adds about 350,000 every quarter.

But 4 in 10 shoppers dropped off at plan selection, roughly 100,000 people a month. Prices, discounts and speeds were hard to compare, especially on mobile.

My role. I led the redesign end to end. I analysed funnel and session data, mapped the full buying journey, synthesised existing research with AI, and prototyped with the Verizon Design System. I worked with product and engineering from goals to rollout.

Key highlights

Clear hierarchy

Price and speed lead every card. Supporting details are one tap away.

Progressive disclosure

Plan details expand in place, so users keep their context. This tested better on mobile.

Hub and spoke

Perks, TV and Home Phone are optional add-ons, which keeps the path to checkout short.

Outcome

Rolling out in phases, measured on how many people move past plan selection. The card and hub patterns are now reused across Home Internet buying.

WiFi Backup

Role
Principal Designer, research to launch
Timeline
6 months, 2025
Team
Product, Engineering, Marketing, Research

When home internet goes down, work, calls and smart home devices stop. Customers needed a simple, reliable way to switch to backup: 7 sessions a month, 24 hours each.

My role. I led design from research to launch over 6 months, with product, engineering, marketing and research. I defined the session model with product, designed the status card and onboarding, and ran usability tests.

Try it

One block for each session

Seven blocks read at a glance. A calm progress bar replaced the countdown, because a ticking timer felt stressful in testing.

Green means a session is running.
Yellow means it is paused and time is saved.
Red means it is about to run out.

Everything else stays neutral, so the one colour that matters stands out.

Key highlights

Session blocks

Seven numbered blocks show used, active and remaining sessions at a glance.

Progress over countdown

A progress bar shows time left. In testing, a live countdown made people anxious.

Semantic colour

Green, yellow and red show active, paused and expiring. Everything else stays neutral.

Onboarding

A short tour, then try it

  1. Works on every deviceOne flow that fits small and large phones, and works across Verizon plans.
  2. A clean lookOne idea per step. The card stays on screen, so people learn on the real thing.
  3. Simple copyShort lines like "Each block is one 24 hour session." No jargon.
  4. Colour with meaningThe tour points out what green, yellow and red mean before people need it.
  5. Try it right awayThe tour ends with a trial session, so people trust backup before an outage.
Outcome

Launched in the Verizon Home app in 2025, available to Home Internet customers across the US.

BlueJeans by Verizon

Video meetings · 2018 to 2023

Meetings, calls and messages in one app

Role
Design Manager, led 6 designers
Platforms
iOS, Android, tablet
Timeline
2018 to 2023

People joined meetings on the move, often on unreliable networks. We had to turn a meetings app into calling, messaging and meetings in one place, without making it harder to use.

My role. As Design Manager, I led a team of 6 (designers, contractors and a content writer) across iOS, Android and tablet. I set the design direction and quality bar, planned the roadmap with product and engineering, and handled hiring and mentoring.

Key highlights

Unified communications

Calling, messaging and meetings in one app. We tested two navigation models, and flat navigation performed better.

Video adoption

Only 30% of users turned on video. We added background blur and virtual backgrounds for signed-in users and guests.

Content sharing

80% of meetings included sharing, so we optimised it for every screen size and orientation.

Faster web join

Permission prompts caused about 30% drop-off. Separating mic and camera prompts and adding recovery flows brought the join rate to 92% for returning users and 82% for new users.

Accessibility

VPAT 2.4 certified against Section 508 and WCAG 2.1.

Conference rooms

Role
Lead Designer, TV and room controller
Timeline
2020 to 2023
Team
Poly, Engineering, Sales, Marketing

Every room is different: size, screens, cameras and languages. Joining from a room had to feel as easy as joining from a laptop.

My role. I led design for the TV interface and a new 12-inch touch controller, shipped with Poly hardware for customers like Facebook, LinkedIn and Red Hat. I worked with Poly, engineering, sales and marketing.

Key highlights

One-tap join and share

Average join time of 10 seconds, including Zoom and Microsoft Teams meetings.

Split-screen layout

Screen sharing happens in 90% of room meetings, and 80% of users preferred People view, so we optimised that layout for TV.

Settings revamp

Restructured navigation and labels, built to scale for new options.

Visual installation guide

A picture-first printed guide, so IT teams can set up a room without a manual.

Outcome

Q Score of 4.85.
